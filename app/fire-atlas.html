      From a question to evidence
      
    
      
      
      1 · Watch
      Press Replay
      Real NASA fire tests launch from Earth into orbit by their actual dates, 2009 to 2021, until a ring of flames circles the planet. Blue are fuel droplets, green are gas flames, orange are fabrics and plastics.
      
      
      2 · Fly
      Pick the Moon base
      The camera flies to the Moon. Only the tests run in air like a Moon base cabin follow: 8 droplets. No fabric or plastic makes the trip. The card says it in one line.
      
      
      3 · Open
      Click any flame
      The test replays from NASA's own numbers: the droplet shrinks, the fabric burns along its length, the flame goes out when the row says it did. The source row is one click below.
      
      
      4 · Switch
      Change the view
      The same flames re-sort into an oxygen-by-pressure map, an oxygen ladder or a timeline. Click empty space on the map to place your own cabin, or use the test list under the stage to find, filter and copy rows.
      
    
      
    
      The detailed oxygen‑by‑pressure chart, export and full review live in the analyst view (Fire Atlas Prototype v2). Mission view is the front door; the analyst view is for researchers. Visual ideas such as the planet stage, mission‑control HUD, live feed and time‑lapse are inspired by ZeroStrike (Olumeyan, Karibiye, Lewandowski, Joshi; HackEurope 2026).       Data. NASA Physical Sciences Informatics, CC0‑1.0: FLEX PSI‑69, CFI PSI‑39, ACME CFI‑G PSI‑159, BASS‑II PSI‑25, SAFFIRE‑I/II PSI‑98/99, ACME BRE PSI‑20. A test "matches" a habitat when its oxygen is within ±3 points and its pressure within ±15%. Solid‑material tables record no pressure, so they match on oxygen alone. CFI‑G inverse flames carry burner oxygen and never match. Mars uses the exploration atmosphere from NTRS 20180004258 (34% O₂, 8.2 psia). Counts are a first pass for the team's two‑person check.
