# Round 2 — beam: design and UX

Read r1-beam, r1-gemini, r1-gpt, r1-grok, r1-kimi, r1-sonnet and the provisional verdict; r1-deepseek was absent at review time. This is a conditional vote on challenges, not an AI-authored solution choice. The human team must originate and choose its approach.

## Agreement, disagreement and concessions

- **Sonnet:** agree that the first screen must communicate the user, data and consequence, and that a pretty demo cannot rescue a broken project. **VERIFIED:** the [submission guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/) says the majority of evaluation is based on the project submission; the demo is at most 30 seconds or seven slides. I concede my first round over-weighted the hero shot: the source, method, accessible controls and usable project link must survive independent review. Disagree that a sorted Flame list must be a weak hero: a visible change in evidence for a changed question, with a source and limitation, can communicate value quickly; this is still untested with viewers.
- **GPT and Grok:** agree that a lunar horizon is a data-validation problem before it is a visual problem, and that FIRMS detections do not establish fire counts or observation exposure. I concede my original 9 for Lunar underpriced the terrain-validation critical path, and my 8 for Hot Spots underpriced harmonization. A crisp diagram can make an invalid result more convincing, not more valid. Do not show an unvalidated correction as a solved sensor seam.
- **Gemini:** agree the Jukebox needs an audio-first route and a real blind/low-vision listener, as well as a non-audio equivalent; no accessibility claim follows from dither, captions or keyboard support alone. Image brightness must be identified as such unless a physical scale is verified. Agree that a child-facing API client requires checking the actual provider terms, not an adult-operated label.
- **Kimi:** agree that AI should earn its place against a simpler baseline; code owns numerical predicates, and raw scientific imagery must reach models un-dithered. A score is an ordinal summary, not a probability that a scientific claim is true; a support-checking model does not independently verify its own input. Test source spans and extraction with people. **DISPUTED:** the provisional verdict's blanket instruction to label Jev probabilities “model confidence”: Kimi documents that Jev confidence and answer probability have different meanings and Noul has no separate confidence. Name the exact output and question; omit numerical claims if the team cannot explain or validate them ([TypeSafe confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence.md)).
- **Grok vs Sonnet on analogs:** both identify the compelling match/mismatch screen; Grok's objection that visual resemblance is not geology is decisive without an expert and sourced common features. Sonnet's GitHub counts are a noisy search proxy, not a demonstrated field size or judge exposure; do not let them outweigh validity. Similarly, Sonnet's SPHEREx blink is a strong first frame, but GPT's epoch, wavelength and registration caveats make a successful endpoint an insufficient go/no-go test.
- **Jukebox dissent:** I disagree with Grok's suggestion that the dithered luminance field should itself drive the synth as a default. Dither alters pixel values and creates texture; derive sound from the original auditable input, and treat dither as optional display. If the team deliberately sonifies a processed image, disclose and test that transformation. Do not imply pixel brightness is a physical measurement.
- **Verdict:** directionally useful but still provisional. Its “calibrated ranking” and fire “harmonized calendar” describe unproved outcomes, not features ready to promise. Its “AI music/voice … watermarked” also conflates disclosure rules: the [submission guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/) specifies visible watermarks for generated images/video and acknowledgement in descriptive text and metadata for generated audio, code and data. Honor the brief's stricter NASA-data and no-logo requirements. The guide also says SI should not come up with the solution idea.

## Revised shortlist and ranked vote

Ordered for this team under the time constraint, weighted toward a truthful, legible user journey as well as the requested stack. Each entry is conditional; failing its gate means reconsidering the direction, not concealing the gap.

| Vote | Challenge | First five seconds / main risk | First two hours: de-risk and decision gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Flame in Freefall** | A question and two visibly different, sourced investigations establish why evidence selection matters. **Risk:** extracted conditions or relevance appear to justify a lunar fire-safety conclusion they cannot support. | Obtain three small PSI/NASA paper records with accessible original excerpts and per-source terms; two people independently check fuel, oxygen, pressure, flow and gravity. Compare a transparent filter with a Jev relevance decision on the same records; call Hydrogen, Jev and the fallback using actual accounts. If evidence cannot be checked or the model adds no useful distinction, narrow to verified evidence browsing and reassess challenge fit. No safety prediction. |
| **2** | **Earth Information Jukebox** | Play a NASA frame and hear a synchronized change, with a short mapping label and transport controls. **Risk:** the sound is an attractive soundtrack or pixel artifact presented as a physical trend. | Fetch one permitted EIC frame sequence and check its source/rights and legend; implement or manually prototype one repeatable original-frame-to-sound mapping, seek/pause and a text equivalent. Ask a listener what changed; seek a blind/low-vision tester and record whether one was available. If units cannot be recovered, label the input as image brightness and do not claim a physical measurement. Verify terms before a child-directed live API client. |
| **3** | **Earth System Trend Detective** | One named variable and region, a readable trend with units and an explicit significant or inconclusive result. **Risk:** a clean chart overstates a p-value affected by seasonality, serial dependence or missing observations. | Obtain one licensed, documented NASA series with time coverage and units; reproduce a known calculation independently, inspect missingness and seasonal/serial structure, and compare an appropriate sensitivity analysis. If no defensible method or sample is ready, show an exploratory series without a significance claim and reassess challenge fit. Jev routing is optional and must beat a simple catalog. |
| **4** | **Lunar Landing Site Navigator** | A site/date and crisp Sun/Earth positions make the visibility trade-off intuitive. **Risk:** a polished spherical horizon is mistaken for terrain-aware visibility. | Get one cached Horizons site/date result and independently check coordinates, time and reference frame; obtain a LOLA-derived terrain profile and compare its horizon with a trusted reference for one site. If that fails, label the view spherical and educational, avoid operational window claims, and reassess challenge fit. Do not equate sunlight with power or Earth visibility with a viable link. |

**Reserves:** MODIS/VIIRS is compelling if an analyst can validate a held-out sensor overlap and observation coverage immediately; otherwise a detection calendar with a visible seam is not proof of the requested harmonization. Earth analogs rises if a planetary geologist can check a purpose-specific, sourced comparison and a genuinely new candidate; a visual match alone is not a site finding. Discarded Equipment remains an accessible human-authored storytelling option if the team has a writer and a separately compliant authoring workflow, with modest AI fit. SPHEREx needs comparable aligned epochs for the prescribed targets before a blink can be trusted.

Across all options: keep labels, axes, source and uncertainty solid and readable; offer original imagery, keyboard navigation, contrast and reduced motion. Use dither only where it does not erase data. Test the first frame and the full project at normal playback size and 200% zoom, credit each actual asset and license, and disclose generated content as required. These are design checks, not a claim of accessibility compliance.
