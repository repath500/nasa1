# Round 1 — Gemini lens: users, global impact, and Google model fact-check

This is a review of human-supplied challenge directions, not a choice of the team's solution. Scores are subjective fit for a 3–5 person, ~40-hour team and the specified stack; they combine user value, plausible adoption, scientific honesty and demo feasibility, not NASA judge scores. Human team chooses the idea. Research reviewed: all 14 challenge briefs and wave-1 findings (gpt-a, grok-a/b, ds1–ds5); claims below distinguish Google documentation from actual tested access. Checks made 14 Nov 2026.

## 2. Shortlist: six challenge options, not a ranked final vote

### MODIS/VIIRS hot spots — historical fire analysts, community planners and land managers

- **Directions:** (a) seasonal calendar comparing one region across years; (b) analyst comparison of a reference period against a recent season with sensor/coverage overlays; (c) regional briefing card that explains detection limitations for an agency or community meeting.
- **For:** [Official challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/modis-and-viirs-hot-spots/) specifically requests a harmonized calendar. [NASA FIRMS](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/) provides the relevant observations. High reuse if outputs can be exported and understood in local languages; a single well-characterized pilot is more honest than claiming global validation.
- **Against:** Detection counts are not burned area, emissions, individual fires, or a forecast. Differences in sensor resolution, overpass, detection thresholds, missing acquisitions and changes in collection confound comparisons; historical anomaly is not an operational warning. Validate a harmonization on overlap periods and use credible uncertainty intervals; verify regional ground truth with a local user.
- **AI:** Hydrogen can extract a cited source's sensor caveat into a reviewed briefing; Jev can classify a documented user question or rank candidate explanations for human review. Neither should invent normalization coefficients, statistically significant trends, warnings, or a probability of ignition. A prettier narrative or arbitrary ranking adds little.

### Earth System Trend Detective — educators, local environmental analysts, journalists

- **Directions:** (a) compare a single variable in two regions with opposing slopes; (b) teach a user to vary baseline and time window while displaying significance and missing data; (c) create a small, reproducible data story with downloadable method and data provenance.
- **For:** [The challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/earth-system-trend-detective/) explicitly requires what, where, magnitude and statistical significance. Community-oriented choices of region and variable make the same instrument useful internationally. [NASA GISS GISTEMP](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/) is an example of a documented NASA temperature product, but verify it is among the chosen challenge's eligible resources before relying on it.
- **Against:** Autocorrelation, seasonality, missingness, multiple tests and choice of start year can turn a compelling plot into a false result; coarse averages do not describe every neighborhood. Preselect one product and method, report units, uncertainty and time coverage, and avoid causal claims.
- **AI:** Hydrogen can turn a user question into a checked choice among documented series and explain a verified result. Jev can route a question to a vetted variable only if it helps more than a search box. Code must calculate trends and significance; model probabilities are not p-values. If a fixed example is all we ship, either model can be omitted.

### Field Shift — farmer/extension partnership, not autonomous agronomy

- **Directions:** (a) compare an existing rotation with two locally curated alternatives using explicit farmer priorities; (b) review a seasonal rainfall/temperature history before a farmer–extension conversation; (c) examine trade-offs between soil cover, water needs and local market constraints for one region.
- **For:** [The challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/adapting-farms-with-nasa-data/) asks for NASA observations, local soil, crop traits and farmer priorities. [NASA POWER](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/) supplies agricultural meteorological data; extension workers can translate regional evidence into locally relevant conversations.
- **Against:** NASA gridded conditions are not a field soil test; locally accurate crop availability, equipment, pests, tenure and markets cannot be inferred from a satellite image. U.S. soil/market sources do not automatically transfer internationally. A pilot requires local agronomic expertise and no claim of yield improvement without validation.
- **AI:** Hydrogen could extract constraints from farmer-supplied notes or cited extension guidance for human confirmation. Jev might prioritize missing information or map explicit priorities to vetted alternatives, but deterministic transparent weights may be better. Neither can fabricate agronomic effects, suitability or risk probabilities. Do not transmit identifiable farm data without a clear consent and data policy.

### Earth Information Jukebox — public, teachers and blind/low-vision audiences

- **Directions:** (a) one visualization with a documented data-to-sound mapping and synchronized controls; (b) an audio-first exploration with transcript, accessible navigation and descriptions; (c) a classroom mapping laboratory letting students adjust a parameter and hear how representation changes.
- **For:** [The challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/the-earth-information-jukebox/) explicitly requests real-time sonification of EIC frames. This can widen access and invite comparison of representations. Seek an actual blind or low-vision tester; screen reader support alone does not demonstrate accessibility.
- **Against:** Visual brightness in a pre-rendered video may be a color scale or compositing artifact, not a physical quantity; labels must identify what is actually mapped. Music generated from a prompt is not auditable data sonification. Give captions, transcript, pause, volume control and reduced motion. Sound-only use cannot depend on a map legend visible only onscreen.
- **AI:** Hydrogen can draft source-grounded descriptions for human review. Jev might route user-selected explanation depth, but scheduling sounds or selecting moods can be ordinary code. Lyria can provide a clearly separate illustrative musical layer, never substitute for the real-time data-to-sound mapping; TTS is separate from Hydrogen. Child-directed deployment must satisfy the Google terms below.

### Flame in Freefall — combustion investigators and spacecraft safety educators

- **Directions:** (a) comparison table of a small set of documented investigations and their experimental conditions; (b) evidence navigator for materials/oxygen levels with clickable source snippets; (c) education view showing how an experimental question and its limitations vary across gravity environments.
- **For:** [The challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/flame-in-freefall/) explicitly asks an SI-powered dashboard to summarize, rank and interpret experiments, so extraction and retrieval have a genuine role. NASA's [Physical Sciences Informatics](https://psi.nasa.gov/) is a challenge-listed starting point. A small curated, traceable set is viable if accessible.
- **Against:** PSI download/auth and machine readability must be tested, not assumed; image brightness is not flame temperature and experiments with different oxygen, pressure, geometry and flow cannot be treated as directly comparable. Safety engineers will not adopt unvalidated results as flight requirements.
- **AI:** Hydrogen can extract conditions and source citations from a few documents, with human review against original tables. Jev can classify whether each record satisfies explicitly stated inclusion criteria and abstain on missing fields; a reproducible numeric filter may be better. Do not present model scores as fire risk or extrapolate an experiment into a safety certification.

### Earth analogs for lunar/Mars exploration — researchers, field teams, local communities

- **Directions:** (a) compare a lunar target against several documented terrestrial field sites for a chosen training task; (b) a side-by-side feature matrix that shows where each analog fails; (c) an evidence explorer of geological or operational constraints for one candidate site and its alternatives.
- **For:** [The challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/permanent-moon-base-locations-and-mars/) asks teams to identify and characterize new analogs using open Earth and planetary data. Clear criteria enable international scientific collaboration and a useful discussion about which features do and do not transfer.
- **Against:** An attractive image is not geological proof. A site is not usable merely because a model ranks it: access permissions, local and Indigenous rights, ecological impact, logistics and existing research are essential. A comparison of known sites alone may fall short of the requested *new* analogs. Select a defensible candidate, cite actual features and call the outcome a hypothesis, not a validated site.
- **AI:** Hydrogen can extract documented attributes from candidate-site papers or compare an image with caveats; Jev can score *evidence availability* or a clearly bounded rubric as an exploratory aid. Explain missingness and show individual dimensions. Don't label scores calibrated scientific probabilities or have the model invent geology; deterministic weighting may be clearer.

## 3. Fact-check and disputed findings

**Status convention:** VERIFIED means I checked the cited primary source for the narrow claim; DISPUTED includes unsupported generalizations, missing qualifications, conflicting claims or items not independently tested. Neither a Google model card nor a source's own calibration claim proves performance on our data. Live account access, latency, pricing tier, licensing for each dataset, and domain accuracy are not tested.

- **VERIFIED — Hydrogen:** [`gemini-4-hydrogen`](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models/gemini-4-hydrogen) is Google's stable ID; text/image/video/audio/PDF in, text out; unified 1,048,576-token window, 65,536 default synchronous output, long decode subject to total window. Structured outputs and function calling are supported; audio/image generation and Live API are not. This confirms grok-a/ds4's division of labor, not performance on scientific imagery.
- **VERIFIED — Carbon:** [`gemini-4-carbon`](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models/gemini-4-carbon) is listed as stable and takes the same five input types with text output; Google describes it as a reasoning model. Its comparative competence is Google's claim, not our independent benchmark. Neither Carbon nor Hydrogen is a documented speech generator.
- **VERIFIED — Decision 1:** [`gemini-decision-1`](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models/gemini-decision-1) takes text, images, video, audio and PDF; 262,144 input tokens, up to 1,000 questions, yes/no, choice and score outputs, zero output tokens, no Batch or Live API. [Pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing#gemini-decision) lists $0.10/million input tokens paid and free-tier availability; each question adds its own input tokens. Google calls outputs calibrated, but **DISPUTED** as an assurance for farm, planetary or safety outcomes without task-specific calibration and labeled cases. A matching question vocabulary does not make Jev and Decision 1 drop-in equivalents: test payloads, output semantics, latency and decisions.
- **VERIFIED — 3.8 Live:** The [`gemini-3.8-live` page](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models/gemini-3.8-live) now confirms the stable ID, real-time audio interaction, text/image/audio input and audio response. This resolves grok-a's earlier uncertainty, but it does not verify the project's entitlement, free quota or child-facing use. The page distinguishes audio output modality from optional output transcription.
- **VERIFIED — TTS:** [Google's speech guide](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/speech-generation) names `gemini-3.8-flash-tts` and `gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts`, text-only in and audio-only out. Treat accent/language quality and accessibility as test questions, not assurances.
- **VERIFIED / DISPUTED — Lyria:** [Google's music guide](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/music-generation) documents `lyria-3.5` from text/images, and the [model page](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models/lyria-3.5) specifies MP3 audio plus lyrics; [pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing#lyria-3-5) lists no free tier and $0.08/song. **DISPUTED:** ds4's unconditional “MP3 or WAV” claim for `lyria-3.5`; the model page specifies MP3, so any WAV conversion is a separate step unless independently verified. [Lyria RealTime](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/realtime-music-generation) is experimental, instrumental, streamed and watermarked; model ID `lyria-realtime-exp`. Its music is not an evidence-preserving sonification merely because parameters were data-driven. Do not assume availability, price or quality without an account test.
- **VERIFIED — terms materially alter global deployment:** [Gemini API Additional Terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms) require API users to be 18+, and prohibit using the services as part of a client directed toward or likely accessed by under-18s. They also require paid services when making API clients available to users in the EEA, Switzerland or UK, and prohibit clinical practice, medical advice and regulated medical-device uses. **DISPUTED:** ds3's suggestion that switching only child-facing runtime calls to Jev, or placing Gemini in a parent/educator-operated mode, automatically solves the restriction: the rule addresses the whole API client and likely access, not just which screen makes a call. A genuinely separate adult authoring workflow may be different, but must be checked under the actual architecture and terms. Verify Jev's own terms separately. For child-focused challenges, prefer human-authored static assets and independently check any external AI service.
- **VERIFIED / DISPUTED — private inputs:** [Google terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms) say unpaid-service content can be used to improve products and should not contain sensitive, confidential or personal information; paid services have different handling, and EEA/UK/Swiss users have a stated data-use exception. Thus grok-a's unqualified “free-tier content is used to improve” needs this geographic qualification. Billing is not consent, medical clearance, or a substitute for privacy review.
- **VERIFIED / DISPUTED — Space Apps rule precision:** [Official submission guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/) requires a visible SI-generation watermark for generated images/video and disclosure of generated audio, code and data in description and metadata. It says Global Award eligibility requires at least one NASA **data or resource**. **DISPUTED:** ds5/grok-a's more restrictive wording that the source must necessarily be a NASA *dataset listed in the chosen challenge*, based on this guide alone. The guide says data **or resource** and does not state that extra qualifier in the cited passage; check all applicable rules and favor an explicitly challenge-listed NASA data product anyway. Honor the brief's stricter minimum of NASA data, no NASA logos, and human-originated idea regardless. Do not label every frame of a non-generated recording as SI-generated solely because AI assisted code; distinguish actual generated images/video and disclose code separately.
- **DISPUTED — Lyria and accessibility claims in ds4:** SynthID or a musical bed does not satisfy Space Apps' separate descriptive-text/metadata audio disclosure, and attractive sound or high contrast alone does not establish accessibility. Test the audio-first flow with the intended users, and document source-to-sound mapping and alternatives.
- **DISPUTED — game/health assurances in ds3:** A confidence number or model-selected triage is not a medical validation. Gemini's clinical-use restriction is explicit; use synthetic, non-identifiable examples and avoid actionable clinical recommendations. Offline-first is a design goal, not proof data never leave a device.
- **DISPUTED — generalized Earthdata licensing in ds5:** [NASA Earthdata data-use policy](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/engage/open-data-services-software-policies/data-use-guidance) supports open sharing but does not mean every NASA-hosted asset, partner dataset, music track, library or imagery file can be treated as CC0. Follow each specific product and code license, source attribution and NASA media rules. Research links are starting points, not blanket license grants.

## 4. Questions before commitment / first research wave

1. Which real user and region will the human team choose, and can one farmer, fire analyst, science educator or accessibility user review a prototype during the event? What action would they change after seeing it?
2. Can the exact NASA challenge-listed product be fetched, cached, cited with version/date/license and shown in the demo? For fire, which overlap years and harmonization baseline can be validated? For Flame, which PSI records and tables are actually accessible now?
3. What real geographic, language, bandwidth and disability constraints apply to the pilot? Can it work with a small cached sample and a legible non-dithered alternative? What local knowledge and permissions are missing?
4. What outcome is the decision model asked to estimate, with what labeled examples, baseline, calibration check, abstention threshold and cost/latency? If a transparent rule or search meets the need, is the model adding value?
5. Can the team's own Jev and Google projects call each named model with the intended input, region, quotas and billing, including Decision 1 schema and fallback? What happens offline or on 429? Do not substitute one score for another without comparison.
6. Does the selected architecture create an API client directed toward or likely accessed by under-18s, users in restricted/unavailable regions, or clinical users? Are paid access, data handling and all providers' terms appropriate? Who reviews the actual deployment?
7. For analogs/farms, which local experts, community representatives, landowners or Indigenous rights-holders must validate claims and access? For fire and trend, how will sampling, resolution and uncertainty be made visible?
8. Can the demo show one verified, source-linked user outcome in 30 seconds with readable labels, non-color-only encoding, motion controls, human-originated idea, at least one NASA data source, complete per-source licenses and applicable SI disclosures?
