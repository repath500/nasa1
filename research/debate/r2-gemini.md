# Round 2 — gemini: users and global impact

This is a comparative vote on challenge areas, not a solution idea or a decision for the human team. I read the available round-one files (beam, gemini, gpt, grok, kimi, sonnet) and the provisional verdict; deepseek's file was absent. The verdict's five-person counts predate kimi and should not be treated as seven-person consensus.

## Agreements, disagreements and concessions

- **Flame:** I agree with beam, gpt and kimi that a small, source-linked evidence comparison has a real user and unusually direct fit to the challenge's SI request. I concede that PSI search and metadata are more accessible than my earlier cautious wording implied: sonnet checked keyless metadata, not necessarily the underlying bytes ([PSI search](https://psi.nasa.gov/geode-py/ws/repo/search?term=combustion&size=5)). Researchers need conditions, provenance and limitations more than a confident-looking rank. A model's relevance score is not flight-safety evidence; kimi's correction that an ordinal Score is not a probability of success matters. A second model checking a quote is not independent verification.
- **Hot spots:** I agree with grok about global reach, and with gpt and beam that downloadability is not harmonization. I lower my earlier 9 as a weekend choice: without coverage and overlap validation, a calendar can mislead land managers. It is historical analysis, not a warning or a burned-area map. The verdict's specific SNPP end date and MODIS transition should remain unconfirmed for this team's slide until checked against current NASA operations sources; the existence of a sensor seam alone does not validate a correction.
- **Trend:** I disagree with sonnet's low ranking driven partly by the line-chart hero and a noisy repository-count proxy. Educators, journalists and local analysts benefit from interpretable magnitude, uncertainty and replicable methods; an existing plotting service does not resolve interpretation. I concede gpt's feasibility caveat: seasonal dependence, missingness, serial correlation and multiple testing cannot be solved by adding a significance badge. A specific product and method need checking. Jev routing is optional, not a statistical validator.
- **Jukebox:** I agree with beam and grok that the sound must encode the source and be explained, and with kimi that generative music or model-selected moods are not necessary to sonification. I concede that genuine access requires keyboard and screen-reader testing with blind or low-vision users, plus captions and a non-audio equivalent; no such test is established. A frame's brightness is not automatically a physical variable. Public reach is an opportunity, not evidence of adoption.
- **Analogs:** I agree with sonnet and kimi that mismatch profiles can make scientific trade-offs legible, but disagree that the reported ~3-repository keyword proxy establishes a clear competitive advantage or that model outputs are calibrated for geology. I concede grok/gpt's deeper validity concern: a visually similar place is not necessarily a new, usable analog; local and Indigenous rights, permissions and ecology matter. Without a defensible candidate and knowledgeable review I would not promote it over the four below.
- **Farms and Lunar:** I concede gpt's and sonnet's implementation constraints: locally grounded crop alternatives and a terrain-validated horizon are both difficult on this clock. Farms has a high impact ceiling only with an actual local agronomist and extension relationship; coarse NASA climate data cannot substitute for a field soil test. Lunar is a strong public explainer, but users must see whether terrain occlusion is included; do not present a smooth horizon as mission-grade. These remain conditional alternatives, not dismissed needs.
- **SPHEREx and story:** Sonnet's five test targets and beam's artifact-centered narrative are compelling public engagement routes. I would not equate a good hero with proven adoption or a complete astronomy pipeline: epoch and alignment checks remain open. Storytelling should be human-authored and sourced. The [submission guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/) says SI must not originate the solution idea; the human team must originate and choose its direction.
- **Model and audience boundaries:** I agree with kimi's baseline test: measure whether AI improves reviewer outcomes over a simple method, and remove it if it does not. Jev confidence is not universally the probability an answer is correct; Decision 1 has different request and confidence semantics, so fallback needs an adapter and testing ([Jev confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence.md), [Google Decisions API](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/decisions-api)). Hydrogen produces text, not voice ([model documentation](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models/gemini-4-hydrogen)). Google restricts Gemini API clients directed to or likely accessed by under-18s; adult authoring does not automatically make a child-facing live client permissible ([terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)). Account access, quotas, domain accuracy and latency remain untested. Cite licenses per actual asset and watermark AI-generated imagery as required.

## Revised shortlist and ranked vote

1. **Earth System Trend Detective** — strongest balance of broad, international educational/analytical use and a defensible, small reproducible workflow; AI adds limited optional assistance. Biggest risk: a misleading significance claim from an inappropriate time-series method. **First two hours:** obtain one challenge-appropriate NASA series with units, missingness and license; have a statistically competent reviewer specify seasonal/serial-dependence treatment and reproduce one documented example. If no defensible method and reference are available, downgrade rather than assert significance.
2. **Flame in Freefall** — a narrow but identifiable research audience, traceable literature and a genuine extraction/triage task. Biggest risk: comparing incompatible experiments or presenting relevance as safety evidence. **First two hours:** retrieve two small actual source documents and PSI records; manually verify oxygen, pressure, fuel, gravity and quoted page for each; compare a transparent human filter with one model extraction/ranking, including an unknown field. If bytes or traceable conditions cannot be obtained, reduce scope or switch.
3. **Earth Information Jukebox** — potentially broad public and accessibility value, with a clear sound-to-data interaction; AI fit is modest. Biggest risk: an attractive soundtrack encoding pixels without a defensible connection to a stated variable. **First two hours:** find one permitted NASA EIC source with values or an explicitly documented pixel mapping, implement and listen to a short deterministic mapping with an intelligible legend, and arrange a representative accessibility test. If only brightness is available, label it as brightness, not a physical measurement.
4. **MODIS/VIIRS Hot Spots** — potentially useful to land managers worldwide if the limitations are legible; the method is the product, not an AI narrator. Biggest risk: false cross-sensor trends caused by detection and coverage differences. **First two hours:** acquire a small overlap-period FIRMS sample for one region, document observation coverage and missing versus zero, and compare sensor counts under a predeclared normalization against held-out overlap months with a knowledgeable reviewer. If coverage or validation cannot be established, do not claim a harmonized trend.

**Conditional reserve:** Earth analogs if a geology reviewer and defensible new candidate are available; Farms if a local agronomist and relevant licensed data are available; Lunar if a checked terrain horizon is feasible. For every option, first-hour API-key and fallback checks are separate from the scientific gates above. No deployment or user adoption is demonstrated by a weekend prototype.
